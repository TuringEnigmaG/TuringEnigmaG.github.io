How the model works

  • Three model tiers, each with a list price and a compute cost per million tokens.
  • Five customer segments react differently: developers are price-sensitive, enterprises much less so, and coding agents sit in between but use the most tokens.
  • Raising one tier's price pushes some usage to the neighboring tiers instead of losing it entirely.
  • Compute cost converts to GPU hours against a fixed monthly capacity.

The four guardrails

  • Gross margin ≥ 55%: the business has to pay for itself.
  • GPU utilization ≤ 90%: demand can't outrun capacity.
  • Active developers ≥ −5%: price can't erode the ecosystem.
  • Revenue ≥ plan: a price change can't shrink the top line.

What it shows (in this toy model)

  • A Frontier price war grows usage but breaks the margin floor first.
  • Raising the Mid price earns more from price-insensitive coding agents, yet loses enough developers to trip the ecosystem guardrail.
  • Cutting the Small price is the cheapest way to grow the developer base.
